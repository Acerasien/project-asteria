# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary users are front-desk staff and hotel administrators working at a single boutique property. They need to understand today's operation at a glance, locate guests and bookings quickly, and complete reservation, check-in, check-out, and room-assignment tasks without ambiguity. Housekeeping staff are a secondary role with focused access to the dashboard and room-status workflows. The expected operating scale is up to 100 rooms, 5–15 concurrent staff, and frequent desktop or tablet use with occasional mobile status checks.

## Product Purpose

Provide one dependable operational workspace for room availability, reservations, arrivals, departures, guests, and housekeeping. Success means staff can safely complete the stay lifecycle, identify rooms requiring attention, and avoid double-booking or invalid status changes without relying on disconnected spreadsheets or verbal coordination.

## Positioning

A calm, operationally precise front-desk workspace that makes the hotel's current state immediately understandable and keeps every room and stay transition safe.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, and trustworthy. The interface should feel focused and professional during busy shifts: familiar enough to learn quickly, dense enough to support real operations, and restrained enough that statuses and actions carry the visual emphasis.

## Anti-references

Avoid decorative SaaS dashboards, glassmorphism, gradients, oversized marketing typography, excessive card nesting, novelty controls, and color used without operational meaning. The application must not feel like a public travel-booking site or a generic analytics template.

## Design Principles

- Make today's operational priorities visible before secondary information.
- Prefer familiar controls and explicit labels over clever interaction patterns.
- Use color for status, selection, warnings, and primary actions—not decoration.
- Preserve context while staff move between reservations, rooms, and guests.
- Prevent invalid operations at both the interface and database boundaries.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. All workflows must support keyboard navigation, visible focus, semantic labels, non-color status cues, adequate contrast, touch targets suitable for tablets, and reduced-motion preferences. Responsive layouts must preserve task clarity rather than merely shrink desktop screens.
